CHAPTER 1: The Fundamental Unit of Life (Cell)

Historical Timeline of Discovery

  • Robert Hooke (1665): Observed dead cork slices under a crude microscope; coined the term "Cell" (Latin: cellula = small room).
  • Anton van Leeuwenhoek (1674): First observed living free cells (bacteria, protozoa, RBCs, sperm cells) using improved lenses.
  • Robert Brown (1831): Discovered and named the Nucleus in orchid root cells.
  • J.E. Purkinje (1839): Coined the term Protoplasm for the fluid matrix inside the cell.
  • M.J. Schleiden (1838) & Theodor Schwann (1839): Formulated the Cell Theory (All plants/animals are composed of cells; cell is the basic unit of life).
  • Rudolf Virchow (1855): Expanded Cell Theory with "Omnis cellula-e cellula" (All living cells arise from pre-existing cells).

Fluid Mosaic Model & Plasma Membrane

Proposed by Singer and Nicolson (1972). The membrane is a semi-fluid phospholipid bilayer with embedded intrinsic and extrinsic protein "icebergs floating in a lipid sea".

Transport Dynamics:

• Diffusion: Spontaneous movement of gaseous molecules (CO₂, O₂) down a concentration gradient (High → Low).

• Osmosis: Movement of water/solvent molecules across a selectively permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.

• Endocytosis: Engulfing food particles (e.g., Amoeba phagocytosis).

Feature Prokaryotic Cell Eukaryotic Cell
Size Generally small (1 – 10 µm) Generally larger (5 – 100 µm)
Nuclear Region Poorly defined, lacks nuclear membrane (called Nucleoid) Well-defined nuclear membrane enclosing chromatin network
Chromosomes Single circular DNA strand without histones Multiple linear chromosomes wrapped around histones
Membrane-bound Organelles Absent (Mitochondria, Golgi, ER all absent) Present (Mitochondria, Plastids, ER, Golgi, etc.)
Ribosomes 70S type (30S + 50S subunits) 80S type in cytoplasm; 70S in mitochondria/chloroplasts

Comprehensive Organelle Breakdown

1. Mitochondria ("Powerhouse")

Double membrane; inner membrane folded into cristae to increase ATP synthesis surface area. Contains own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes (Semi-autonomous organelle).

2. Plastids (Chloroplasts)

Present in plant cells. Contains thylakoids stacked into grana and fluid stroma. Site of light and dark reactions of photosynthesis. Semi-autonomous.

3. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

SER: Synthesizes lipids/steroids; detoxifies drugs in liver cells. RER: Studded with ribosomes; synthesizes membrane proteins.

4. Golgi Apparatus

Discovered by Camillo Golgi. System of membrane-bound cisternae. Functions in modifying, packaging, and sorting proteins; forms lysosomes.

5. Lysosomes ("Suicide Bags")

Single membrane vesicles containing hydrolytic digestive enzymes active at acidic pH. Autophagy and destruction of foreign pathogens.

6. Vacuoles

Bound by single membrane called Tonoplast. Occupies up to 90% plant cell volume; maintains turgidity and stores cell sap.

CHAPTER 2: Tissues (Plant & Animal Tissues)

Plant Tissues Master Classification

A. Meristematic Tissues: Actively dividing cells with thin cell walls, dense cytoplasm, and prominent nuclei. Lacks vacuoles.

  • Apical Meristem: Tips of roots and shoots → Primary growth (Length).
  • Intercalary Meristem: At bases of leaves or nodes → Internodal growth.
  • Lateral Meristem (Cambium): Girth/thickness expansion → Secondary growth.

B. Permanent Tissues: Derived from meristems after differentiation.

  • Simple Permanent: Parenchyma (storage/photosynthesis), Collenchyma (flexibility, pectin corner thickening), Sclerenchyma (mechanical strength, lignin deposition, dead at maturity).
  • Complex Permanent: Xylem (water transport: tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma, xylem fibers) & Phloem (food transport: sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibers).

Animal Tissues Master Classification

1. Epithelial Tissue: Protective lining.

  • Squamous: Flat cells (blood vessels, lung alveoli, mouth lining).
  • Cuboidal: Cube-like cells (kidney tubules, salivary ducts).
  • Columnar / Ciliated: Pillar-like cells (respiratory tract, intestine).

2. Connective Tissue: Cells embedded in intercellular matrix.

  • Fluid: Blood (RBCs, WBCs, Platelets, Plasma) & Lymph.
  • Supportive: Bone (Calcium/Phosphorus matrix) & Cartilage (Chondrocytes).
  • Fibrous: Tendons (Connects Muscle to Bone) & Ligaments (Bone to Bone).
  • Binding/Fat: Areolar (Repair) & Adipose (Fat storage insulator).

3. Muscular Tissue: Striated (Skeletal/Voluntary), Smooth (Visceral/Involuntary), Cardiac (Heart/Involuntary & Branched).

CHAPTER 3: Improvement in Food Resources

1. Crop Variety Improvement

Achieved through Hybridization (intervarietal, interspecific, intergeneric crossing) or genetic modification to incorporate desirable traits:

  • Higher crop yield.
  • Biotic (pests, diseases) and Abiotic (drought, salinity, heat) resistance.
  • Wider adaptability & desirable agronomic characteristics (tallness/profuse branching for fodder, dwarfness for cereals).

2. 16 Essential Plant Nutrients

Plants require 16 elements derived from air, water, and soil:

• Air: Carbon, Oxygen.

• Water: Hydrogen.

• Macronutrients (Soil): N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S (6 total).

• Micronutrients (Soil): Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl (7 total).

3. Animal Husbandry & Aquaculture

Cattle Farming: Bos indicus (Cow) & Bos bubalis (Buffalo). Milch animals (milk-producing) vs Draught animals (farm labor).

Poultry & Apiculture: Cross-breeding Indian (Aseel) with Exotic (Leghorn) breeds. Beekeeping using Italian bee Apis mellifera for high honey yield and gentle nature.

Composite Fish Culture: Rearing 5-6 fish species in a single pond (e.g., Catla - surface feeder, Rohu - middle zone, Mrigal/Common Carp - bottom feeder).

Chapter 5: Cell Question 1 of 100

Which cell organelle is known as the "suicide bag" of the cell due to the presence of hydrolytic enzymes?

Scientific Explanation:

Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes formed by RER. When cell metabolism is damaged or cell ages, lysosomes burst and digest their own cell.

What is Plasmolysis?

Scientific Definition

Plasmolysis is the shrinkage or contraction of the protoplasm of a plant cell away from its cell wall when placed in a hypertonic solution due to exosmosis.
